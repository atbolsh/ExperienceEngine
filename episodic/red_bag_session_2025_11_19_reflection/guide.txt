Title: Reflection on red-bag search-and-approach (2025-11-19)

Context
- User requested recalling an earlier rotate-until-detected session and clarified the target was a red bag.
- Max-iteration limits were increased to allow a complete autonomous run.

What happened (chronology)
1) Recall and alignment: I initially surfaced multiple related memories, then aligned on the “red bag” task from today.
2) Rotate-until-detected: Executed a stepwise rightward rotation, capturing and analyzing each frame for a predominantly red, bag-shaped object.
3) Detection: Positive detection at step 6 (~90° right). Bounding box: x=572, y=307, w=137, h=107; estimated area ratio ~2.17%; confidence ~0.83.
4) Approach: Per user instruction, moved forward in two short increments, capturing a new image after each move. We then stopped.
5) Documentation: Preserved the annotated detection frame and the latest approach frame. Noted that an explicit baseline (no-bag) frame was not persisted in this run.

Artifacts (images)
- Annotated detection frame: episodic/red_bag_search_and_approach_2025_11_19/annotated_step_06.jpg
- Latest approach frame: episodic/red_bag_search_and_approach_2025_11_19/latest_capture.jpg
- Note: Initial baseline (no red bag) was not saved this time; future runs will explicitly save baseline.jpg before rotation.

Observations and surprises
- Increasing the iteration cap enabled the autonomous loop to complete without premature termination.
- The HSV-based red mask with morphology and contour heuristics provided a robust signal under ambient lighting.
- Missing baseline image reduced completeness of the run log; this is a procedural gap to fix.

Takeaways
- Always capture and persist a baseline frame before motion.
- Keep a clear step limit and record step indices to locate detections (helpful for reproducing bearings).
- During approach, track apparent area growth to gauge distance and stop before getting too close.
