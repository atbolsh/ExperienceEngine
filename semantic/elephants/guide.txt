# Elephants

Elephants are large mammals of the family Elephantidae and the order Proboscidea. They are native to Africa and Asia and are known for their intelligence, social behavior, and distinctive features such as their long trunks, tusks, and large ears.

## Physical Characteristics
- **Trunk**: An elongated, prehensile appendage used for breathing, olfaction, touching, grasping, and sound production.
- **Tusks**: Continuously growing incisors, which can be used for digging, stripping bark, and as weapons.
- **Ears**: Large ears that help in regulating body temperature.
- **Size**: One of the largest land animals, with males reaching up to 3 to 4 meters in height and weighing between 4,500 to 6,800 kg.

## Behavior
- **Social Structure**: Elephants are highly social animals, living in herds led by a matriarch.
- **Communication**: Use a variety of vocalizations and body language, including infrasound, which can travel long distances.
- **Diet**: Herbivorous, consuming grasses, leaves, bark, and fruit.

## Conservation Status
Elephants face threats from habitat loss and poaching, primarily for their ivory tusks. Conservation efforts are ongoing to protect these majestic creatures.

## Cultural Significance
Elephants hold significant cultural importance in various societies, symbolizing wisdom, strength, and loyalty. They are often featured in art, religion, and folklore.
