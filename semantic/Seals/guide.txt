Seals are marine mammals that belong to the Pinnipedia group, which also includes sea lions and walruses. There are two main families of seals: the Phocidae, commonly known as earless or true seals, and the Otariidae, known as eared seals, which include sea lions and fur seals.

Seals are adapted to life in the water, with bodies that are streamlined for efficient swimming. They have flippers instead of limbs, which help them in swimming, and they can hold their breath for extended periods while diving.

Diet: Seals primarily feed on fish and squid, but their diet can vary based on species and habitat. They are known to be opportunistic feeders.

Habitat: Seals are found in various environments, from polar to tropical waters. They often haul out on land or ice to rest, molt, or give birth.

Reproduction: Most seals give birth on land or ice. The pups are typically born with a thick layer of blubber and often a coat of fur to keep them warm.

Communication: Seals communicate with each other through vocalizations, body language, and sometimes physical contact.

Threats: Seals face various threats, including climate change, habitat destruction, pollution, and hunting. Some species are endangered due to these factors.

Conservation: Efforts to protect seals include marine protected areas, hunting regulations, and rehabilitation programs for injured or stranded animals.

Interesting Facts:
- Some seals, like the elephant seal, can dive to depths of over 1,500 meters and stay underwater for more than an hour.
- The harp seal is known for its striking white coat as a pup, which changes as it matures.
- Seals have specialized whiskers that help them detect vibrations and movements in the water, aiding in hunting.
